# Interval-method sensitivity for H1-H5 (reviewer B)

Generated by `python3 -m ForestSearch.stats_sensitivity` from the confirmatory CSVs (seeds 200-207, 8 instances, 300 episodes each). **This is a robustness analysis, not a re-registration:** the verdicts in `hypotheses.md` and the paper remain the preregistered ones.

Methods. (a) **Cluster bootstrap**: preregistered, `analyze_curve.paired`, B=2000, percentile; instances resampled, then episodes within instance. (b) **t**: paired per-instance mean differences (8 clusters), 95% t interval, df=7. (c) **BCa** 95% on the 8 instance means (B=20000). (d) **TOST** (H1 only): standard alpha=0.05 equivalence test against +-0.03, equal to the 90% t interval inside +-0.03; p = max of the two one-sided t p-values. (e) **Holm** over the 12 non-trivial H1 points (lambda in {1,2,3} x 4 cells) for the superiority direction (ORACLE > IMPLICIT, one-sided t); the four lambda=0 points are exactly 0 by Prop. 1 (identical trajectories), so H1 is 12 tests, not 16.

**Why the bootstrap is conservative.** The preregistered two-stage bootstrap resamples instances and then episodes inside each resampled instance. The variance of the mean of per-instance means already contains the within-instance (episode) noise once; resampling episodes again adds it a second time. Its intervals are therefore wider than the instance-level ones (width ratio below), the opposite of the undercoverage the Limitations section warns about. The t interval has its own weakness: with 8 clusters it relies on approximate normality of instance means and the nominal 95% level is not guaranteed. BCa on 8 points is also unreliable in the tails. No method here is the 'true' one; the table shows which verdicts are method-dependent.

## 4. Reading

- H2, H3, H5 (the substantive effects) pass under all three interval methods in every cell.
- H1: see section 2. The failing preregistered cell (6x6 t0, lambda=2) passes standard TOST; at the same time the uncorrected t test for ORACLE > IMPLICIT is nominally significant there, and does not survive Holm over 12 points. The defensible wording is 'no H1 point is significant after Holm correction', not 'never significant'.
- H4: pass/fail pattern across cells depends on the method (section 3); report it as method-dependent.
- The 0.03 equivalence margin (about 4-5% relative to P(IMPLICIT) of 0.58-0.79) is a judgement; effects the paper calls meaningful under H2 are 0.03-0.06, the same order, so H1 equivalence is a statement about 'no larger than the smallest harm we report'.
